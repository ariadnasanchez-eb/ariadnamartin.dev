The Delivery Health Check

A 45-minute conversation about how your team really works, followed by a short written report. I look at five areas, the ones that usually start to creak first when a team grows.

Delivery predictability

Do commitments hold? Where does work wait?

Product–Engineering alignment

Does the team know why it builds what it builds?

Prioritisation

Is there one clear priority, or is everything urgent?

Team effectiveness

Can the team decide without escalating everything?

Process & metrics

Are metrics used to learn? Do retros change anything?

What you get

  • A score from 1 to 10 for each area
  • What's working, so you protect it
  • The main risks slowing you down
  • Three concrete recommendations to start with

  1. Pick a timeBook a slot that suits you in my calendar.

I've been on the inside of the team

I've worked at team and organisation level, connecting business strategy, people, process and technology. So I don't arrive with a framework to install. I look at how your team actually works and help fix the part that's slowing it down.

  • Helped scale a tech organisation from around 40 to 70+ people
  • Cut cycle time by 30% and made delivery more predictable
  • Reduced recurring defects by about 25% through quality and testing changes
  • Led teams through hiring, growth and promotion with 100% retention over two years
  • Worked on digital products with 50M+ monthly impressions

Three reasons the change sticks

  1. People and system, together. In my experience, most delivery problems are half process, half people: unclear ownership, decisions that wait for one person, managers stretched too thin. I've managed teams, not just processes, so I work on both sides at once.
  2. Your team owns it. Less process for the sake of process. We agree a few concrete changes with the people doing the work, instead of installing a framework from outside.
  3. It's measured. We take a baseline before we start, cycle time, predictability, unplanned work, and look at it again at the end. You see whether it worked, not just whether it felt better.
